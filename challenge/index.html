01The claim

OSF authenticates by a secret key K = (p₀, â, ω, t₀). At time t it emits a tag H( s_K(t) ‖ nonce ). Security rests on the key being secret — the algorithm is fully open (Kerckhoffs's principle). We claim: seeing any number of past tags does not let you forge the next one. Prove us wrong.

02How this challenge works

  1. We generated a fresh secret key K and published … real authentication transcripts {t, nonce, tag} — exactly what a network eavesdropper would capture.
  2. We picked one fresh target (t*, nonce*) and published only commit_tag = SHA-256(tag*). The correct tag* and the key K stay secret.
  3. You win by producing tag*. Paste it below — your browser checks SHA-256(your tag) == commit_tag. No server, no telemetry, nothing to trust but math.
  4. commit_k = SHA-256(K) is published now so we cannot swap keys later. When the challenge ends we reveal K; anyone can then recompute every transcript and tag* to confirm the challenge was honest.
